# DevAwesome SEO check-up and first expansion

Date: 2026-08-22  
Scope: repository, production URLs, existing free and paid recovery evidence, current portfolio contract  
Launch state: production preview, crawlable noindex  
Primary project assignment: Contextter (unchanged)

## Decision

DevAwesome has a coherent standalone job: tested developer-tool guidance, executable field tests, and small browser-local engineering utilities. It is not ready for index release because an independent technical reviewer and former-brand/identity clearance remain undocumented. The first expansion therefore improves the product and its future search architecture without advertising URLs in the sitemap or removing noindex.

No new DataForSEO or other paid request was made. The existing 2026-08-22 recovery export was read as historical evidence only.

## Technical findings before implementation

### P0 — launch gate

The live site is correctly crawlable but held from indexing. Removing the meta/header directive or publishing URLs in the sitemap would conflict with the documented reviewer and identity gates. No change authorised.

### P1 — metadata and URL consistency

Open Graph metadata was absent across the production inventory. Slash variants created duplicate 200 surfaces for slashless canonical URLs. Both are global, measurable defects with low-risk central fixes.

### P1 — architecture drift risk

Page inventory, canonical rules, and sitemap eligibility were not represented by one registry. A future index release could therefore omit a page or expose the wrong page without a type or build error.

### P2 — practical next step

The site explained and demonstrated its method, but did not help a reader declare their own test. A guide plus local builder closes that loop without requiring accounts, analytics, storage, a new backend, or unverified market claims.

### Not proven

No authenticated Search Console performance export, analytics dataset, conversion log, or query-level impression data was available. Search demand, cannibalisation based on impressions, CTR, ranking potential, and conversion impact remain unproven.

## First expansion acceptance criteria

- The two new routes have unique title, description, H1, canonical, OG/Twitter metadata, one primary job, visible breadcrumbs, truthful schema, and natural parent/sibling links.
- Builder entries remain in memory only and are sent nowhere.
- Tool behavior is route-specific and not loaded by the homepage or quiz.
- The sitemap is generated from the route registry but stays empty during the hold.
- Robots remains crawlable and does not advertise an empty hold sitemap.
- All canonical pages retain meta and response-header noindex.
- Existing selective legacy URL decisions remain unchanged.
- Build, field-test runners, route/canonical/robots/sitemap/redirect/404 checks, broken-link QA, browser console, mobile overflow, keyboard flow, and critical accessibility checks pass before deployment.


## Implemented first expansion

- Added a typed 14-route registry with one user job, cluster, evidence state, and future search eligibility per canonical URL.
- Derived the sitemap from that registry. Because the launch gate remains closed, the built sitemap contains zero URLs and robots does not advertise it.
- Added global Open Graph and Twitter title, description, URL, site-name, and type metadata.
- Added visible breadcrumbs plus BreadcrumbList data to the guide, tool, and field-test detail pages.
- Added the practical guide at /guides/reproducible-developer-tool-tests.
- Added the browser-local builder at /tools/developer-tool-test-plan and its static JSON Schema v0.1.
- Added permanent canonical slash redirects while preserving /guess-the-programming-language/ as the documented trailing-slash target.
- Kept all legacy restore, redirect, and 404 decisions unchanged.
